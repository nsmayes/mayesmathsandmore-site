Mental maths tricks

Pip’s Trick Box has 24 shortcuts for doing maths in your head. Each one appears in the school year whose maths it uses, with a short lesson and practice. Here is how each one works, in Pip’s words.

  • 24 tricks
  • Years 1–11

Near doubles

From Year 1

If you know your doubles, you know lots more facts for free. 6 + 7 is a near double: the numbers are next to each other.

Double the smaller number, then add 1. Double 6 is 12, and one more is 13.

Make ten first

From Year 1

Tens are easy to add to. So when a sum goes past ten, make ten first.

8 + 5: 8 needs 2 more to make 10. Split 5 into 2 and 3. 8 + 2 = 10, then 10 + 3 = 13.

Adding and taking away 9, 11, 19 and 21

From Year 2

Adding 9 is fiddly, but adding 10 is easy. So add 10, then take away 1: 47 + 9 = 57 − 1 = 56.

Adding 11? Add 10, then 1 more. Taking away 9? Take away 10, then add 1 back, because you took away one too many.

Times 10 and 100: add zeros

From Year 3

Here is a quick trick: when you multiply a whole number by 10, just add a zero on the end. 36 × 10 = 360. Times 100? Add two zeros: 7 × 100 = 700.

Why it works: every digit moves one place to the left, and the zero fills the empty ones place.

Double, double again

From Year 3

Times 4 is double, then double again. 23 × 4: double 23 is 46, double 46 is 92.

Times 8? Double three times. To double a big number, double the tens and the ones separately: double 36 = 60 + 12 = 72.

Split it up to multiply

From Year 3

Big numbers are easier in pieces. 7 × 34: split 34 into 30 and 4.

7 × 30 = 210 and 7 × 4 = 28. Add them: 210 + 28 = 238.

Count on to take away

From Year 3

When two numbers are close, count up from the smaller one instead of taking away. 403 − 396: from 396, 4 more makes 400, then 3 more makes 403.

The difference is 4 + 3 = 7. No exchanging needed!

Times 5 and divide by 5

From Year 4

5 is half of 10. So to multiply by 5, multiply by 10 and then halve. 46 × 5: 460, half of 460 is 230.

To divide by 5, do it the other way: double, then divide by 10. 340 ÷ 5: double is 680, ÷ 10 is 68.

Times 9 and times 11

From Year 4

9 lots is one lot less than 10 lots. So to multiply by 9, multiply by 10, then take away one lot. 34 × 9: 340 − 34 = 306.

Times 11 is one lot more than times 10: 34 × 11 = 340 + 34 = 374.

Prices ending in 99p

From Year 4

£4.99 is just 1p less than £5. So round up to whole pounds, then take off the pennies.

£4.99 + £2.99: £5 + £3 = £8, take off 2p: £7.98. Three at £1.99: 3 × £2 = £6, take off 3p: £5.97.

Times 19, 21, 99 and friends

From Year 5

To multiply by 19, think of it as 20 lots, take away one lot. Multiply by 10, then by 2, then take away one of the number.

23 × 19: 23 × 10 = 230, × 2 = 460, then 460 − 23 = 437. Times 21? Add one lot instead: 460 + 23 = 483.

Times 50 and times 25

From Year 5

50 is half of 100, so × 50 is × 100, then halve. 36 × 50: 3,600, halved is 1,800.

25 is a quarter of 100, so × 25 is × 100, then halve and halve again. 36 × 25: 3,600 → 1,800 → 900.

Halve one, double the other

From Year 5

If you halve one number and double the other, the answer stays the same. Keep going until you reach a round number.

16 × 25 = 8 × 50 = 4 × 100 = 400. And 14 × 15 = 7 × 30 = 210.

The times 11 digit trick

From Year 5

A magic one: to multiply a 2-digit number by 11, write its digits apart and put their sum in the middle. 43 × 11: 4, then 4 + 3 = 7, then 3. The answer is 473.

If the middle sum is 10 or more, carry the 1 to the left: 68 × 11 → 6, 14, 8 → 748.

Divisibility tests

From Year 5

You can tell if a number divides exactly without doing the division. 2: the last digit is even. 5: it ends in 0 or 5. 10: it ends in 0.

3: add the digits; if the total is in the 3 times table, so is the number. 9: the digits add up to a multiple of 9.

Percentages from 10%

From Year 6

10% is easy: divide by 10. 10% of 340 is 34.

Then build what you need: 5% is half of 10%. 20% is double 10%. 15% is 10% + 5%. 25% is a quarter. 1% is ÷ 100.

Squaring numbers ending in 5

From Year 7

To square a number ending in 5, multiply the tens digit by the next number up, then write 25 on the end.

35²: 3 × 4 = 12, then 25: the answer is 1,225. 75²: 7 × 8 = 56, so 5,625.

Swap the percentage

From Year 7

8% of 25 looks hard. But x% of y is always the same as y% of x, so swap them: 25% of 8 is a quarter of 8, which is 2.

It works because x% of y means x × y ÷ 100, and you can multiply in either order.

Dividing by 0.5, 0.25 and 0.2

From Year 8

Dividing by 0.5 asks how many halves fit. There are 2 halves in every 1, so ÷ 0.5 is the same as × 2: 7 ÷ 0.5 = 14.

In the same way, ÷ 0.25 is × 4 (four quarters in 1), ÷ 0.2 is × 5 and ÷ 0.1 is × 10.

Either side of a round number

From Year 9

49 × 51: both numbers are 1 away from 50. The answer is 50² − 1² = 2,500 − 1 = 2,499.

It works for any distance: 47 × 53 = 50² − 3² = 2,500 − 9 = 2,491.

One multiplier for a percentage change

From Year 9

To increase by 15% you end up with 100% + 15% = 115% of the amount, so multiply by 1.15.

To decrease by 15% you keep 100% − 15% = 85%, so multiply by 0.85.

Reverse percentages: divide by the multiplier

From Year 10

A coat costs £96 after a 20% rise. What did it cost before? Do not take 20% off £96!

The new price is 120% of the old one, so new = old × 1.2. To go back, divide by the multiplier: £96 ÷ 1.2 = £80.

Squaring numbers near 100

From Year 10

97²: 97 is 3 below 100. Take the 3 off again: 97 − 3 = 94, so start with 9,400. Then add 3² = 9: 97² = 9,409.

Above 100 it works the other way: 104² → 104 + 4 = 108 → 10,800, add 4² = 16: 10,816.

Estimate with 1 significant figure

From Year 11

To estimate, round every number to 1 significant figure (its first non-zero digit), then work it out in your head.

4.87 × 31.2 ≈ 5 × 30 = 150. 6.12 × 48.7 ÷ 2.03 ≈ 6 × 50 ÷ 2 = 150.

Questions parents ask

Why learn mental maths tricks?

Shortcuts make everyday sums quicker and build number sense. Each trick is taught once the maths behind it has been learned, so it makes sense rather than being a rule to remember.
