What Year 10 GCSE maths covers

These are the 68 Year 10 GCSE maths topics in the app, grouped by the areas of the National Curriculum for England. Under each topic is the curriculum’s own wording. In the app, each area is a place on the Year 10 map.

Number – structure and calculation Circuit Square in the app

  • Ordering and inequality symbolsOrder positive and negative integers, decimals and fractions; use the symbols =, ≠, <, >, ≤, ≥
  • The four operationsApply the four operations, including formal written methods, to integers, decimals and simple fractions (proper and improper), and mixed numbers – all both positive and negative; understand and use place value (e.g. when working with very large or very small numbers, and when calculating with decimals)
  • Inverse operations and priorityRecognise and use relationships between operations, including inverse operations (e.g. cancellation to simplify calculations and expressions; use conventional notation for priority of operations, including brackets, powers, roots and reciprocals
  • Primes, factors and multiplesUse the concepts and vocabulary of prime numbers, factors (divisors), multiples, common factors, common multiples, highest common factor, lowest common multiple, prime factorisation, including using product notation and the unique factorisation theorem
  • Listing strategies and the product ruleFoundation and HigherApply systematic listing strategies including use of the product rule for counting
  • Counting with the product ruleHigherApply systematic listing strategies including use of the product rule for counting
  • Powers and rootsFoundation and HigherUse positive integer powers and associated real roots (square, cube and higher), recognise powers of 2, 3, 4, 5; estimate powers and roots of any given positive number
  • Estimating powers and rootsHigherUse positive integer powers and associated real roots (square, cube and higher), recognise powers of 2, 3, 4, 5; estimate powers and roots of any given positive number
  • Index lawsFoundation and HigherCalculate with roots, and with integer and fractional indices
  • Fractional and negative indicesHigherCalculate with roots, and with integer and fractional indices
  • Exact answersFoundation and HigherCalculate exactly with fractions, surds and multiples of π; simplify surd expressions involving squares (e.g. √12 = √(4 × 3) = √4 × √3 = 2√3) and rationalise denominators
  • SurdsHigherCalculate exactly with fractions, surds and multiples of π; simplify surd expressions involving squares (e.g. √12 = √(4 × 3) = √4 × √3 = 2√3) and rationalise denominators
  • Standard formCalculate with and interpret standard form A × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer

  • Inverse and composite functionsHigherWhere appropriate, interpret simple expressions as functions with inputs and outputs; interpret the reverse process as the ‘inverse function’; interpret the succession of two functions as a ‘composite function’

How Pip explains it

Every topic starts with a short lesson from Pip the owl, in small steps. These are Pip’s own words from three Year 10 lessons.

Then children practise, with a hint when they need one and a step-by-step “Show me how” when they are stuck. Questions get harder as they improve.

Year 10 practice papers

Three practice papers in the layout of each exam board (AQA, Edexcel (Pearson), OCR and Eduqas), at Foundation tier (grades 1 to 5) and Higher tier (grades 4 to 9): one non-calculator paper and two calculator papers, 45 minutes each. They are marked the way an examiner would, with method marks for working.

Questions parents ask

Is this Year 10 GCSE maths practice really free?

Yes. Every Year 10 GCSE maths topic is free, with no adverts, no in-app purchases and no subscription. There is no account to make: open the app and choose Year 10.

What do children learn in Year 10 GCSE maths?

Year 10 GCSE maths in England (Key Stage 4 (GCSE), ages 14–15) covers number, fractions, algebra, graphs and probability. Mayes Maths & More has all 68 topics, in 7 areas, each with a lesson, practice at three levels and a quiz.

Which exam boards and tiers are covered?

Practice papers follow the layout of AQA, Edexcel (Pearson), OCR and Eduqas, at Foundation tier (grades 1 to 5) and Higher tier (grades 4 to 9). Questions carry marks, and children can see how an examiner would mark each one. See GCSE maths practice.

Can my child practise topics from another year?

Yes. A grown-up can change a child’s school year at any time, and can search every topic in every year and set any one of them as a special task.
